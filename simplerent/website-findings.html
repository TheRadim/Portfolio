I have collected the detailed design findings behind my main recommendation here. The proposal remains platform-independent; implementation details can follow once the scope is agreed.

I base these recommendations on the desktop page review and the October design, mobile, motion and sitemap findings. I have kept the limits of those checks visible. Checkout, payment and private accounts have not been tested.

Homepage: give visitors a useful next step

The opening photograph and lime headline communicate filmmaking, but the hero offers no direct browse or search action. Contact us is the strongest header button. Equipment and kits appear further down the page; the featured gear is a narrow selection of anamorphic lenses.

I recommend keeping the production imagery, adding a short offer/location line, and making search and Browse equipment the obvious starting points. Browsing stays date-free. Ask for rental dates when adding equipment, prefill the next item, and allow different periods per item in the basket. An optional availability filter supports earlier date checking; rental days remain distinct from pickup and return. Show actual equipment types soon after the introduction, then kits and a short explanation of the rental process. Use representative featured products or clearly name the reason for a specialist selection.

The mobile review reports a booking banner, cookie bar and promotional modal competing on first visit. I recommend removing that competition: one interruption at a time, no immediate promotional popup, and booking constraints explained where dates are selected. Do not hide essential rental conditions merely to make the hero cleaner.

Navigation: remove choices that do not help people choose

The camera route I inspected goes through Shop, Equipment categories, Cameras & accessories, Cameras and Cine before products. Search and menus can shorten that route, but the category gateways still add avoidable work. Kits also has separate editorial and product-category entry points.

The proposed site has five main sections. Rent gear contains one catalogue for rental and purchase items; studios and services each get their own place, while rental help and learning come together.

Search, Equipment list, Account and Cart stay in the header. About, Contact and policies stay in the footer, with useful help links beside booking decisions. Make Book equipment the main action. Rent gear opens directly onto the six groups below, without an intermediate page containing three broad buttons.

Give each product one main subcategory, then connect it to relevant kits and compatible extras. Lens mount, brand and specifications should be filters, not extra layers of navigation. Audio recorders belong with audio; monitor-recorders with monitors. Rental batteries belong under power, disposable batteries under consumables. Items can be available to rent, buy or both; only show the options SimpleRent actually offers.

Keep the route to a product short: Rent gear → group → subcategory → product, with direct search and menu shortcuts. The latest sitemap review reports 765 products across 94 categories, with some addresses up to seven levels deep. If the proposed structure is taken forward, I recommend mapping the full catalogue into it. Ownership and effort remain to be agreed; this is not the immediate next deliverable.

/rent/ is the working address. Confirm whether /gear/ would better describe a catalogue that also contains things to buy. The five sections are the proposed direction; their final labels and active offers still need Frederik’s review. Generators should only appear if SimpleRent confirms an actual offer.

Search and listings: show products sooner and improve comparison

Sony FX6 search returned the expected camera kits, but an accessory appeared first. The results field did not retain the query. The sampled Cine listing exposed no filtering or sorting and showed little beyond picture, name, day rate and actions.

I recommend exact-model matches first, accessories clearly grouped, a retained search query and category-appropriate filters. The agreed direction is the full relevant filter set below; completeness of product data is a launch task, not a reason to silently drop those filters. Once dates are selected, show relevant period prices and availability; undated browsing should still work.

Cards need a useful differentiator for Basic and Advanced configurations and a consistent VAT/duration label. Prefer See dates & price if the next step is choosing a rental period. The October review reports add-to-cart URLs behind Reserve buttons, but that does not establish what date defaults/session logic is applied. Validate the resulting behavior safely before describing it as a defect.

Every listing and search result should have useful filters and one consistent sort menu. Keep exact model matches ahead of accessories, retain the typed query, and recognize names such as FX6, Sony FX6 and ILME-FX6 as the same search. Suggestions can include products, gear groups and kits.

Across the catalogue: available for my dates, Rent/Buy, a price range and brand. Rental prices use a price-per-day slider in DKK with the VAT basis clearly stated. In Buy mode, show purchase prices instead. Date availability applies to rental items; purchase items need their own stock indication.

Gear group Useful filters
Cameras and optics Lens mount; focal-length range; sensor size/coverage; lens type and T-stop; resolution and recording format
Lighting and grip Wattage; colour/daylight/bi-colour/RGB; battery power; light type and mount
Audio and monitoring Microphone type and channels; screen size; brightness in nits; connections; recording capability
Support, motion and power Payload; battery mount; cable connector and length
Live and streaming Inputs and connections, including SDI, HDMI and NDI where relevant
Media and consumables Card type and capacity

Use range sliders for values such as focal length, price, payload, screen size and cable length, with editable number fields. Only show filters relevant to the selected products. Wattage describes power, not directly how much light a fixture produces; label it honestly and add comparable measured output only where reliable specifications exist.

Sort by: Recommended (default), Most rented in the last 90 days, Price low to high, Price high to low, Newest, and Name A-Z. Recommended can combine staff choices, popularity and availability once dates are selected. Most rented must use actual bookings; buy-only items should not pretend to have rental popularity.

Selected filters appear as removable labels above the results, with Clear all. Show result counts, keep filter and sort choices in the page address so links can be shared and the back button works, and update results without a full reload. On mobile, open filters in a panel from the bottom with a Show … items button. When nothing matches, explain which filters to loosen and offer relevant alternatives.

Product pages: make contents, dates and total work together

The inspected Sony FX6 Basic kit already has date fields, stock text, quantity, multi-day prices and a selected-period total. These capabilities should be preserved. The missing clarity is what the kit includes and how Basic differs from Advanced. Technical description and videos do not replace a list of included items.

I recommend complete contents with quantities, clear exclusions, key specifications in a table and genuinely compatible accessories. Keep dates, stock status, total and action together. A sticky booking panel is worth testing on desktop, with a more compact mobile treatment that does not obstruct content.

The Blazar review also reports inconsistent price typography, a missing VAT label on the first price line and widely spaced specifications. These reinforce the need for a reusable product layout, though exact content differs by product.

The stock display already includes words and numbers, so it is not solely colour-based. Improve its connection to selected dates and announcement of updates rather than adding an icon as the entire solution.

Kits: keep the useful storytelling and clarify the package

Interview Kit A connects a filming result to equipment, setup guidance and add-ons. This is one of the site's useful existing ideas. I recommend building on it rather than presenting kits as a new feature.

The visible contents block lists four camera/audio/lens items while the description mentions lighting. Confirm the complete manifest and show quantities, exclusions and required experience. Qualify the hero price by duration and VAT. Link each kit to its components, and products back to relevant kits.

Editorial and booking views should have one recognizable identity. That is a customer-facing recommendation, not an instruction to delete the Film Kits records. Start with curated kits and optional additions; defer a dynamic builder until shared stock and substitutions are understood.

Delivery and useful additions

I recommend pickup or delivery as a basket choice, with any delivery charge shown before confirmation. It does not belong in Crew and production services as an item to add. Product-level eligibility can be shown where useful; coverage, scheduling, charges and restrictions need confirmation and a plain-language explanation in Resources and support.

I recommend unobtrusive compatible extras on product pages: suitable media for a camera, a boom arm for a microphone, and a stand or extension cable for a light. When several selected items overlap a complete kit, offer that kit with clear contents and a price comparison. Two relevant items can be enough to make the suggestion useful; the exact matching rule can be tested during design. Never replace items automatically or imply savings without checking the prices. This is a curated suggestion, not a commitment to build a full kit configurator.

How renting works: explain the process before the exceptions

The FAQ holds useful rules, but important decisions are scattered across accordions. The two-day-before-pickup banner and one-day-ahead booking statement need an agreed interpretation. They may reflect different operational expectations rather than a broken date rule.

I recommend a short rental sequence followed by clearly labeled topics for prices, pickup/return, self-service access, insurance, damage, payment and cancellation. My working interpretation is two days preferred, with one-day requests sometimes possible; Frederik must confirm. These topics should be directly linkable from relevant parts of the booking journey. Review the Covid section and explain “Sluse” in plain English after confirming the facility type.

The five-minute stock hold is a public promise. Show its start, expiry and recovery clearly if it remains. No cart hold, checkout or payment flow was tested here.

Accounts, credit and business customers: preserve value first

The public guidance describes future credit on qualifying bookings and staff-approved invoice arrangements for returning businesses, including EAN guidance. Private account screens were not inspected.

I recommend preserving existing credit and payment permissions, then designing an account overview around upcoming rentals, collection details, lists, history, invoices and credit. A repeat-booking shortcut should recheck current stock and prices. New rewards, loyalty tiers or automated business approval are separate proposals, not migration essentials.

Studios and services: use information suited to the offer

Facilities currently advertises a studio, edit suite, office and flex desk. Crew/post services are described in an article. Use separate Studios and spaces and Crew and production services sections. Explore automated room booking and a simple upper-floor plan, subject to room calendars and accurate measurements. Confirm crew roles and generators before listing them.

Show acoustics, size, capacity, access, equipment and additional costs on room pages. The sampled Simple Studio is described as not sound isolated. Confirm whether the proposed content/podcast studio is the same room or another offering. Room hours and staffed contact hours may legitimately differ; explain them rather than silently making them match.

Learn: organize what already exists

Guides & Articles already has useful content and pagination. It mixes tutorials with hiring and service information. I recommend separating evergreen learning, company news and service destinations, then linking guides to relevant equipment.

The existing content hub needs clearer organization. Root-level article URLs do not by themselves prevent a coherent learning experience. Resources and support combines rental guidance and learning. I propose roughly nine complete practical guides, linked to the six core kits, covering setup, lighting, LOG, grading, studios, audio and backup. Prepare missing pages privately. Remove the one-episode podcast from promotion unless renewed; review early-2024 courses and remove stale offers. A video library is a later option. Prefer a social link over an automatic Instagram feed unless the feed serves a clear purpose.

Visual design: refine the identity and improve legibility

I recommend keeping black, white, purple and lime, unless Frederik chooses otherwise. Preserve authentic production photography and use minimal motion with subtle expressive touches. Consolidate inconsistent colours, type sizes, buttons and price blocks. Improve the relative hierarchy of page headings, section headings and product titles without prescribing arbitrary pixel sizes.

The October critique reports weak contrast for lime text on light backgrounds, white text on bright green/lime controls and faint placeholders. It reports ratios including 1.32:1 for lime on white and 1.56:1 for a white-text Load more button. These values were not remeasured here. The practical recommendation is clear: check actual text/background combinations and use dark text on light/lime controls, with lime text primarily on dark surfaces.

Choose typography for readability, language coverage and consistency. A licensed webfont is an option; a system stack is not inherently a defect. Image overlays need testing against the actual photographs rather than assuming one opacity guarantees contrast.

Mobile, keyboard and motion: fix the basics before adding polish

The October review reports small header targets around 20px and absent visible focus, plus overlapping first-visit overlays at 375px. These extend the original desktop-only review, but are recorded findings rather than checks repeated during this review. Prioritize larger usable controls, clear keyboard focus, readable form labels and layouts that retain dates and price context. The contact phone control was exposed as a numeric stepper in the desktop inspection and should support telephone-format entry.

Check headings by template. The homepage logo was an H1; the inspected FX6 and studio products also had product-name H1s. The heading issue therefore needs checking by template rather than treating every product page as affected. FAQ controls were exposed as headings with plus/minus text; keyboard and screen-reader interaction still need direct verification.

The stylesheet review reports slow menu/flyout fades, a one-second equipment-list fade, sticky-header animation and layout-property transitions. I recommend fast, restrained feedback; easy interruption; reduced-motion support; and removing unnecessary header/FAQ animation. Avoid animating all properties indiscriminately. Exact durations are implementation choices, and CSS declarations alone do not prove dropped frames. Animating grid rows is not automatically free of layout cost. Card lifts and badge bumps should only be added if they improve feedback.

Content and URL cleanup: check before retiring anything

The October review identifies possible test/duplicate kit URLs and a chatbot-related page in the sitemap. Review their content, use, incoming links and search visibility before deciding whether to keep, redirect or remove them. Sitemap inclusion is not proof of search-engine indexing. Duplicate-looking slugs may contain different material; do not delete them solely from their names.

Plan shorter routes for the replacement site if useful, with individual redirects to appropriate equivalents. Avoid promising that changing URL length alone will improve rankings. Search Console data, if available, can help identify important landing pages before migration.

The earlier quick-win timings relate to the old WordPress scope and are outdated as delivery estimates for the replacement platform. I retain the findings because contrast, focus, clear actions and responsive menus still matter. Any fixes to the existing site would be separate work to agree, rather than the assumed next stage.
