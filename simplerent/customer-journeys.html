Customer journeys

I recommend the following five routes through the proposed website. They describe intended experiences, not results from customer interviews or completed booking tests. The observed friction comes from the public-page review; account, checkout, delivery and room-booking steps remain proposals to validate with SimpleRent.

1. Experienced professional

Objective: find a known model, check the configuration and book it quickly.

Entry: search on the homepage or a direct product link.

Proposed route: search for model or alias > compare relevant configurations > inspect included items > choose rental dates and quantity > review compatible extras > basket > choose pickup or delivery > confirm total and booking details.

The FX6 search reviewed placed an accessory ahead of the camera kits, and the Basic/Advanced differences were not clear enough. I recommend exact-model matches first, explicit configuration differences and a compact booking area. Browsing does not require dates. After dates are selected for one item, I recommend carrying them forward to the next item, with the option to change them.

To validate: search ranking across more models, compatibility data and the booking system's handling of different rental periods per basket item.

2. Beginner or occasional renter

Objective: choose a complete setup with enough guidance to use it confidently.

Entry: Production kits or a practical guide linked from search.

Proposed route: choose a shoot type > see the intended result and required experience > review complete kit contents and exclusions > read the setup guide > choose dates > add optional extras if useful > review total, pickup/delivery and rental rules > confirm booking.

The Interview Kit already connects a production outcome with useful guidance. Its visible contents need checking against the description, which also mentions lighting. I recommend keeping that storytelling while showing exactly what is included, what the customer supplies and where to ask for help.

To validate: kit manifests, guide accuracy and which setup questions customers most often ask. The assumption that kits reduce uncertainty is a design proposal, not a measured outcome.

3. Small production crew

Objective: assemble compatible equipment for a shoot without missing essential parts.

Entry: Rent gear, search or a shared filtered listing.

Proposed route: find the main equipment > narrow by relevant specifications > review compatible media, support, audio or power > add items with carried-forward dates > compare a relevant kit when selected items overlap it > adjust quantities and per-item dates in the basket > choose pickup/delivery > review and confirm.

The current category route adds several broad gateways before products. I recommend direct access to the six gear groups, useful filters and shareable result addresses. Quiet “You might also need” suggestions can help complete the setup. If a microphone and boom arm overlap an interview audio kit, I recommend showing the kit's contents and price difference as an alternative. The customer decides whether to switch; nothing is replaced automatically.

To validate: actual component compatibility, quantities, kit stock, delivery eligibility and whether a proposed kit genuinely fits the selected dates and needs. I am not proposing a full collaborative production-planning system at this stage.

4. Returning customer

Objective: manage an upcoming rental or repeat a previous equipment selection.

Entry: Account or Equipment list in the header.

Proposed route: sign in > see the next rental and collection/delivery information > open a previous rental or saved list > reuse the selection > choose new dates > recheck current availability and prices > review credit and eligible payment options > confirm.

Public guidance describes credit and approved business invoice arrangements, but private account screens were not reviewed. I recommend protecting those existing benefits and making upcoming rentals, invoices and saved lists easy to find. A saved list is not a reservation; repeating a booking must check stock and price again.

To validate: existing account workflows, migration of balances and permissions, invoice access, and which repeat-booking features the replacement system supports. This journey is based on documented capabilities and proposed improvements, not a tested account flow.

5. Customer renting a space

Objective: choose a suitable room and understand access, equipment and cost.

Entry: Studios and spaces in the main navigation.

Proposed route: compare active spaces > inspect size, acoustics, facilities and access > locate the room on a simple floor plan > review included equipment and optional kit/crew links > check dates and total if direct booking is supported, or send an enquiry > receive clear confirmation and arrival instructions.

Facilities are currently grouped with equipment. The reviewed Simple Studio page describes a 30 m² room without sound isolation. I recommend a dedicated space route with room-specific information, rather than assuming that a room is suitable for podcast recording. Crew and post-production remain a separate service destination.

To validate: active rooms, accurate floor plans, calendars, access hours, acoustic limitations and who confirms a booking. Automated room booking is an option to explore, not an existing capability I have verified.

Shared principles

I recommend keeping search and browsing available without dates, making rental and purchase prices distinct, and showing the full commitment before confirmation. Delivery belongs in the basket, with coverage and charges confirmed by SimpleRent. Clear help links can support every journey without turning Contact into the dominant action.

The interface parts show how a few key parts of these routes could look. The priorities separate core needs from improvements and later options. Neither document commits to a development scope or delivery schedule.
